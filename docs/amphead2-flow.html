AmpHead 2 — how it all works

one pane, one local engine, no host application · the amber line is audio, the teal line is control
1
You press the switch. It reads SET UP only while the engine is closed. Pressing it opens the interface and parks the master at −60 dB — no sound yet — and loads back the last chain you played. Once the engine is up that same switch reads GO LIVE, because setup has already done its job. It is one control in two states, never two buttons.
2
You pick a capture and a cab. The two knobs at the top of the face list the files on this machine, by folder. The tone's name is just those two names.
3
You press LIVE. That arms the chain and brings the master up to −6 dB, a level measured so a −18 dBFS DI peaks at −2.9 dBFS instead of clipping.
4
Audio runs on its own thread. Guitar in → capture → tone → cabinet → master → out. Same 512-frame block, one duplex stream, 10 ms round trip, about 15% of one core.
5
You turn the knobs. Each move is a route, a verb, and a real change on the audio thread — and the engine reports the value back, so the face shows what the engine holds, not what you asked for.
6
SILENCE is always one press away. It parks the master and takes all three stages out of the path. Pressing LIVE afterwards puts exactly those stages back and says so.

Two processes, one rule: the pane never owns the audio. The engine is a separate process holding the device, so a slow click, a redraw, or a closed chat can never stall the callback. Everything the face shows is something the engine answered — the mute button, the meter, and the numbers on the knobs all come back from the same place.
